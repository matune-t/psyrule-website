# PsyRule — Brand Voice Guide

> Internal reference. Use this before writing or reviewing any PsyRule-facing copy: website, in-product strings, weekly reports, support replies, social, email.

Last updated: 11 May 2026
Owner: founder

---

## 1. Brand personality

If PsyRule were a person, they'd be the funded trader two desks over who used to blow up the same way you do — three challenges in, finally figured it out, and now talks about it without sugar-coating. They will not tell you that "discipline is key." They will tell you what your re-entry time was on Tuesday at 11:02 and what it cost you the last six times.

Knowledgeable, specific, slightly weary of the genre's clichés, but on your side. Never a coach. Never a guru. Definitely not a hype account.

---

## 2. Audience

**Primary:** funded-account or challenge-stage prop traders, mostly forex and futures, who have already tried journaling and rule-blockers and found them insufficient. They know the strategy isn't the problem. They are sick of being told to "trade your plan."

**Secondary:** retail traders who recognize the same patterns in their own behavior.

**They expect:** to be talked to like adults. Specifics over slogans. No motivational posters. No "trader hustle" energy. No emojis on the website.

**They distrust:** anything that sounds like a course, anything that promises outcomes, anything that uses the word "edge" as a verb, anything that calls them "rockstar."

---

## 3. Voice attributes

Four attributes, fixed across all channels. Tone (section 5) is the dial that moves.

### Direct

- **We are:** plain-spoken, short sentences when they land, second-person ("you"), confident about specifics
- **We are not:** blunt to the point of cold; not curt with customers; not dismissive of the difficulty
- **This sounds like:** "You're 27 seconds from placing a third trade after two stop-outs. Your re-entry time is a third of normal."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Studies show that revenge trading is a common pitfall among retail traders. Consider taking a break."

### Specific

- **We are:** ruthless with numbers, timestamps, named patterns, dollar costs, sequences
- **We are not:** fake-specific (no invented stats, no "9 out of 10 traders…"); we don't quote anonymous "studies"
- **This sounds like:** "Rule #07 — your revenge loop. Hold 90 seconds. Avg cost the last 6 times this fired: −$312."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Many traders find that pausing after a loss can help."

### Founder-confessional

- **We are:** willing to say "I lost three challenges in a row to the same loop." First-person singular is allowed in the manifesto, founder notes, and email replies.
- **We are not:** performatively vulnerable; we don't trauma-dump for engagement
- **This sounds like:** "I failed prop firm challenges before I admitted it wasn't the strategy. It was me, at 11:02am, after a stop-out. PsyRule is the tool I wish had existed then."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "After my journey of self-discovery as a trader, I realized…"

### Anti-coaching

- **We are:** dismissive of generic advice, blockers, and motivational language — *as a positioning point*, not as a personality
- **We are not:** sneering at competitors by name; not personally rude
- **This sounds like:** "Generic advice ('size down after a loss') is easy to ignore. A pattern named with your trigger, your sequence, and your historical cost is harder to."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Other tools are garbage. Use ours."

## 4. Messaging pillars

Every page reinforces these. If a piece of copy doesn't connect to at least one, ask what it's doing there.

1. **It's behavioral, not strategic.** The strategy isn't the problem. Your behavior at 11:02 after a stop-out is.
2. **Personal, not generic.** Your trigger, your sequence, your historical cost — not a textbook.
3. **Cost is visible.** Discipline is invisible; we put a dollar number on each loop so the trade-off is concrete.
4. **Friction, not blocking.** We show the rule and the cost; the decision is yours. Override is always available, with a cooldown.

Sub-positioning vs. category: *not a journal, not a blocker — a behavioral risk layer*.

## 5. Tone spectrum by channel

The voice doesn't change. The tone dial does.

| Channel | Tone | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page hero | Confident, slightly provocative | "You already know it's behavioral. Now do something about it." |
| In-product intervention card | Calm, specific, never alarmist | "Pause. This is your revenge loop." |
| Weekly report (email) | Quiet, observational, dollar-anchored | "You broke even on P&L. Three rule breaks did the damage." |
| FAQ | Patient, candid, no marketing-speak | "No. PsyRule shows the rule and the cost; the decision is yours." |
| Support reply | Warm + specific; first person allowed | "Looked at your last 14 sessions — the false-positive you flagged is from a single-instrument rule I'll soften." |
| Pricing | Plain, no urgency theater | "Locked for life for the first 100 customers." |
| Legal (Privacy, Terms) | Plain-language summary first; precise underneath | "Plain-language summary first, full notice below." |
| Social (LinkedIn / X) | Punchy, specific, single-claim | "Your revenge loop has a timestamp. Mine was 11:02." |

**Situational dials:**

- Product launch: dial up specificity, not energy. No exclamation marks.
- Incident or outage: dial up plain-spoken accountability. State what happened, what we're doing, what to expect.
- Bad news (price increase, deprecation): dial up directness. State it in the first sentence. No "we regret to inform" preamble.

## 6. Style rules

### Grammar & mechanics

- **Oxford comma:** yes ("fast, specific, and honest").
- **Headings:** sentence case with italic emphasis on the verb/object that matters. *"From pre-session check-in to **live intervention**."*
- **Contractions:** yes (we're, can't, don't, won't). Always.
- **Em dashes:** spaced — like this — not unspaced.
- **Numbers:** numerals for anything quantitative ("30 seconds", "100 customers", "11:02"). Spell out only when starting a sentence and a numeral would look odd.
- **Percent:** symbol "%", no space ("28%").
- **Currency:** "$" symbol attached, no space, minus sign for losses ("−$312", not "-$312" or "$-312"). Use the proper minus glyph (U+2212).
- **Time:** 24-hour for in-product timestamps ("11:02"); 12-hour with no zero-padding for prose ("11:02am" is fine but prefer "11:02").
- **Dates:** "11 May 2026" in legal/footer; "Apr 14 – Apr 20" in product strings.
- **Exclamation marks:** never on the website. Rarely in product. Twice would be a crisis.
- **Emojis:** never on the website. Sparingly in email greetings. Never in the product UI.
- **ALL CAPS:** only for monospace eyebrows ("WHY THIS EXISTS"). Never for emphasis.

### Emphasis

- *Italics* via `<em>` for the verb, object, or contrast word that carries the sentence. "It's about *psychology*."
- **Bold** for inline weight on data and named entities ("**−$312**", "**Rule #07**").
- One italic emphasis per heading. Two is too many.

### Formatting

- **Headings:** Fraunces serif, italic green accent on the emphasis word.
- **Body:** Manrope, 16px base.
- **Mono treatment:** JetBrains Mono for eyebrows, tags, timestamps, technical data. It is the "I am being precise" font. Don't use it for prose.
- **Links:** descriptive ("See the full comparison →"). Never "click here." Trailing arrow "→" on internal CTA links is fine.
- **Lists:** kept short. If a list is longer than six items, the surrounding prose is probably doing too little work.

### Punctuation oddments

- Middle dot "·" is the metadata separator ("Trader: you · FTMO 100k · sent Sunday 18:00").
- Right-arrow "→" is the sequence operator in pattern cards ("First loss → shorter wait → larger size → second loss").
- The colon does heavy lifting in headlines. The semicolon almost never appears.

## 7. Terminology

### Wordmark

- **PsyRule** — written as a single capitalized word in body copy, ALT text, page titles, and emails.
- Visual logo treatment uses italic green "Rule"; that's a wordmark choice, not a spelling choice. Never write "Psyrule", "PSYRULE", or "Psy Rule" in body copy.

### Preferred terms

| Use this | Not this | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| sign up *(verb)* | signup *(verb)* | "signup" is acceptable as a URL slug only |
| log in *(verb)* | login *(verb)* | "login" is acceptable as a noun ("Log in" CTA) |
| pre-session check-in | pre-session checkin / check in | hyphenate both halves consistently |
| behavioral risk layer | behavioral journal / journaling | journals are the thing we are *not* |
| intervention | nudge / alert / popup | we say "intervention"; it's deliberate |
| pattern | habit / tilt / bias | "tilt" is in the audience's mouth, not ours, except in quotes |
| rule | guardrail / restriction / block | we don't block, we set rules |
| funded trader, prop-firm trader | prop trader (alone) | "prop trader" can mean a firm employee; specify |
| challenge | evaluation | most firms use "challenge"; match the firm's word in product |
| MetaTrader 4 / 5, cTrader, NinjaTrader | MT4/MT5/cTrader/NT *(in body prose)* | full names on first use, slashes acceptable in dense feature lists |
| weekly behavioral report | weekly summary / digest | "behavioral" is the differentiator; keep it |

### Avoid

- "Edge" used as a verb ("get an edge"). "Edge" as a noun is acceptable in trader contexts ("your edge window").
- "Rockstar", "ninja", "warrior", "beast mode", and any other phrase that would belong on a hype account.
- "Disruption", "revolutionize", "game-changing", "next-gen".
- "Hack" your trading.
- "Just" used as a softener ("just one click", "just check"). Cut it.
- "Easy" — what's easy varies. Use "simple", "fast", or quantify ("takes 30 seconds").
- "We're excited to announce." We're not.
- Trading-outcome promises of any kind. We don't say "stop losing", "pass your challenge", "guaranteed", or "proven."

### Inclusive & accurate language

- Use "they/them" for unknown individuals. Don't gender the generic trader.
- Avoid culture-specific idioms in the product (we have international users).
- Avoid framing trading losses moralistically ("you screwed up", "bad trader"). The framing is: a pattern fired, and here's its cost.

### Naming competitors

- Reference by exact brand spelling: **TradeGuard**, **Edgewonk**, **Tradezella**, **Tradis**.
- Never disparage. Never editorialize per row. Let the ✕ and ✓ speak.
- Always keep "Competitive data based on public feature listings as of [month] [year]" in the same view as any comparison table.
- Maintain dated evidence (screenshots) per ✕ in a private folder.

### Cost and outcome language

- Always present cost figures as **modeled**, not measured-on-customers, until we have real customer aggregates published with a methodology page.
- Standard hedge: "modeled from a small private beta and internal historical data, not customer averages."
- Real customer numbers, when we have them, are attributed: "Trader: K., FTMO 100k, Apr 2026."

## 8. Legal & compliance baseline

Every public page must:

1. Link to **Privacy** and **Terms** in the footer.
2. Carry the standard risk disclaimer: *"PsyRule is a behavioral analytics tool. It does not provide trading, financial, or investment advice. Trading involves risk; past performance does not predict future results."*
3. Hedge modeled-cost figures with the standard phrasing on first appearance.
4. Avoid trading-outcome promises.
5. Date competitive claims and keep evidence on file.

Founder-credibility statements about trading results ("currently profitable", "now profitable") are not used on the public site. Acceptable phrasing: "trading a funded account full-time" or "now trading on a funded account."

## 9. Examples — apply the voice

### Headline rewrite

❌ "Unlock your trading potential with AI-powered psychology coaching."
✅ "You already know it's behavioral. Now do something about it."

### CTA rewrite

❌ "Get started today — it's super easy!"
✅ "Join the waitlist."

### Bug-fix release note rewrite

❌ "🎉 We're thrilled to announce a major update that revolutionizes how you experience PsyRule!"
✅ "Faster pattern detection on revenge loops. Two false-positive cases reported by beta users — both fixed. Notes below."

### Outage update rewrite

❌ "We are currently experiencing some technical difficulties. Our team is working hard to resolve them. We apologize for the inconvenience."
✅ "Sync is down for MT5 accounts as of 14:32 UTC. Cause: broker API change. ETA to fix: ~2 hours. Trades placed in the meantime will backfill once sync returns."

### Refund email rewrite

❌ "We're sorry to see you go! As per our policy, we are happy to issue a refund within the eligibility window."
✅ "Refund processed — it should land in 3–5 days. If PsyRule didn't surface a pattern you'd call yours, I'd like to know which one we missed. Reply if you're up for it. — [name]"

### Pattern naming rule

When PsyRule names a user's pattern in the product, the construction is always: **trigger → behavior shift → outcome**, with the user's actual data plugged in.

Template: *"After [trigger event] you [behavior change] within [time], averaging [size change]. The next trade has lost [N] of [N] times. Modeled cost: [$amount]."*

Example: *"After a stop-out you re-enter within 4 minutes, sized 35% above plan. The next trade has lost 5 of the last 6 times. Modeled cost: −$312."*

## 10. The 30-second voice test

Before publishing any piece of copy, ask:

1. **Specific?** Does it have a number, timestamp, name, or named pattern? If not, can it?
2. **Second-person?** Does it talk *to* the trader, not *about* trading?
3. **Cliché-free?** Have I used "edge", "discipline", "psychology" without earning them?
4. **Substantiated?** Is every claim either modeled-and-disclosed or grounded in the user's own data?
5. **Hedged where it counts?** Modeled numbers are labeled. Outcome claims don't exist.
6. **Dial appropriate?** Calm in interventions; quiet in reports; confident on the landing page. Same voice.

If five of six are yes, ship it. If three or fewer, rewrite from the verb.
